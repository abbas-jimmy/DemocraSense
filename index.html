01 — Foundation

What is Voting?

Voting is the act of expressing your preference or choice in an election. It is the cornerstone of democracy — the system where the people hold the power to decide who governs them and what policies shape their lives.

When you vote, you are participating in a collective decision-making process that has been fought for by generations. Your vote is your voice in government — it is constitutionally protected and legally guaranteed.

Elections happen at local, state, and federal levels — from your city mayor to the President. Each election shapes laws, budgets, schools, healthcare, and your daily life.

🏛

Federal Elections

President, Vice President, Senators, House Representatives

🏙

State Elections

Governor, State legislators, Attorney General

🏘

Local Elections

Mayor, City Council, School Board, Judges

📋

Ballot Measures

Direct votes on laws, taxes, constitutional amendments

03 — The Process

How Elections Work

A step-by-step visual guide to the entire election cycle.

1

Candidates Announce

Politicians declare their intention to run. They form campaign committees and begin raising funds. Primary challengers within a party also emerge at this stage.

~12–18 months before Election Day
2

Primary Elections

Each party holds primary elections where registered voters choose their party's candidate. There are "open" and "closed" primaries depending on the state.

Spring/Summer of election year
3

Party Conventions & Debates

Parties officially nominate their candidates. Public debates give voters a chance to compare candidates on key issues directly.

Summer of election year
4

Voter Registration Deadline

Most states require you to register by a set deadline (15–30 days before the election). Some states offer same-day registration. Check your state's deadline!

15–30 days before Election Day
5

Early Voting & Mail-In Ballots

Most states offer early in-person voting (1–4 weeks before Election Day) and mail-in/absentee ballots. This makes voting more accessible.

~2–4 weeks before Election Day
6

🗳 Election Day!

Federal elections are held on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Polls are typically open 6 AM – 8 PM. If you're in line before closing, you have the right to vote!

First Tuesday of November
7

Vote Counting & Certification

Ballots are counted by election officials. Results are certified by state authorities. For presidential elections, the Electoral College formally votes in December.

Days to weeks after Election Day
8

Inauguration / Taking Office

The winner is sworn into office. For the President, Inauguration Day is January 20. Local and state officials may have different start dates.

January 20 (Presidential)
04 — How to Vote

Step-by-Step: Casting Your Vote

Step 1
📝

Register to Vote

Go to vote.gov or your state's election website. You'll need your ID or SSN. Register well before the deadline.

Step 2
🗺

Find Your Polling Place

Use the address on your registration. You can look it up on your state's election site or at vote.gov/polling-place-locator.

Step 3
🪪

Bring Valid ID

Many states require photo ID. Accepted forms: driver's license, passport, state ID, student ID (varies by state). Check your state's requirements.

Step 4
✉️

Request Mail-In Ballot (Optional)

If voting by mail, request your ballot early. Fill it out carefully, sign the envelope, and return it by the deadline — mail it early!

Step 5
🗳

Go to the Polls

Arrive during voting hours (typically 6 AM – 8 PM). If you're in line when polls close, you have the right to stay and vote.

Step 6
☑️

Mark Your Ballot

Follow instructions on the ballot carefully. You can leave races blank if you choose. Review your selections before submitting.

Ways to Vote

🏫 In-Person (Election Day)

Visit your assigned polling place on Election Day. The most traditional method.

⏰ Early Voting

Cast your ballot in person at designated locations days or weeks before Election Day.

📬 Mail-In / Absentee

Request a ballot by mail, fill it out at home, and return it by mail or drop box.

💻 Online (Limited)

A few states offer online ballot return for military and overseas voters. Not widely available.

05 — Choosing Candidates

How to Decide Who to Vote For

Evaluating candidates wisely is a skill. Here's a framework to help you make an informed choice.

🔍

Research Their Record

Look at what they've actually done, not just what they say. Past votes, past statements, and past actions are more reliable than campaign promises.

  • Check voting records on congress.gov
  • Read their official website's policy positions
  • Look at endorsements and donors
📰

Use Multiple News Sources

Read from diverse, reputable sources. Cross-check claims. No single outlet has the full picture.

  • Check fact-checkers: PolitiFact, FactCheck.org
  • Read across the political spectrum
  • Distinguish news from opinion/editorial
⚖️

Compare on Issues That Matter to You

What issues affect your life? Education, healthcare, housing, economy? Find out where each candidate stands.

  • Use ISideWith.com to match your views
  • Watch debates with an open mind
  • Consider both short and long-term impacts
🚩

Watch for Red Flags

Certain behaviors signal a candidate may not be trustworthy or effective in office.

  • Vague or contradictory promises
  • Appeals to fear without solutions
  • Refusal to engage with evidence
  • History of corruption or ethical violations
🤝

Consider Their Character

Leadership qualities matter. Look for honesty, accountability, and respect for democratic institutions and the rule of law.

  • Do they accept responsibility for mistakes?
  • Do they respect the opposition?
  • Do they uphold constitutional norms?
🧠

Think Independently

Your vote is private and personal. Don't let peer pressure or social media dictate your choice. Make an informed, independent decision.

  • Verify before you share
  • Be skeptical of viral claims
  • Your values are your own
06 — Rules & Regulations

Voting Rules You Must Know

🔒 Your Rights at the Polls

  • You have the right to vote free from intimidation
  • You can request help filling out your ballot
  • If in line when polls close, you may still vote
  • You can vote a provisional ballot if your registration is questioned
  • You have the right to a translated ballot in many areas

🚫 What's NOT Allowed

  • Voting more than once in the same election
  • Voting as someone else (voter impersonation)
  • Campaigning within 100 feet of polling places
  • Taking photos of your marked ballot (in some states)
  • Bribing or threatening voters
  • Voting if you are not a US citizen

📅 Key Deadlines (Vary by State)

  • Voter registration deadline: 15–30 days before election (some states: same day)
  • Mail-in ballot request deadline: 1–7 days before election
  • Mail-in ballot return: Must arrive by Election Day (some states allow postmarked by Election Day)
  • Early voting window: 4–45 days before Election Day

🆔 ID Requirements

  • Strict Photo ID states: Must show valid photo ID
  • Non-strict states: Photo ID requested but alternatives exist
  • No ID states: Signature or name lookup is sufficient
  • Always check your state's requirements at vote.gov

⚖️ Felony Conviction Rules

  • Most states: Rights restored after sentence is completed
  • Some states: Rights restored after parole/probation
  • Maine & Vermont: Never lose voting rights (even while incarcerated)
  • Iowa & Kentucky: Permanent disenfranchisement (unless individually restored)

📣 Reporting Problems

  • Call the Election Protection Hotline: 1-866-OUR-VOTE
  • Contact your local election board
  • Report intimidation to the Department of Justice
  • Document issues with photos/notes if safe to do so
